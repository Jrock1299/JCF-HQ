## 2. Jerry's P&L pipeline — three defects, not one

The reported symptom was "all 90 closed trades record `pnl: 0.0`". Investigation
found that was the visible third of a fully broken pipeline. See [[jerry]] for
the detail; summary:

1. **The close-path race.** `_mark_closed` computed P&L only inside
   `if symbol not in positions:` — evaluated microseconds after submitting a
   market close order. Alpaca still lists the position until that order fills,
   so the branch never executed. 90/90 closes affected.
2. **Server-side exits never recorded.** Bracket SL/TP orders fill at the
   broker and never call `close_position()`, so those trades stayed `open`
   locally forever. Local file said 39 open; broker had 8.
3. **Entry price was never the fill.** `buy_stock` recorded the pre-trade
   snapshot as cost basis — wrong on **119 of 126** records, understating the
   book by ~$5.1k. This one matters most: even with defects 1 and 2 fixed,
   every P&L number would still have been wrong.

**Fix.** Both entry and exit prices now come from the broker's own fill record
via `_resolve_order_fill()` (`filled_avg_price`, already VWAP across partial
fills). Added `reconcile_closed_positions()`, called at the top of every
trading tick, to close out broker-side exits. Where a fill cannot be
established the trade records `pnl=None` + `pnl_status=UNVERIFIED_*` — never a
fabricated `$0`, which would drag win rate and expectancy toward zero. This
applies Rule #9 at the *write* side, not only at the grading side.

**Verification.** A real paper round-trip on Alpaca (buy 20 F, close, inspect):
entry $14.43 from `order_fill`, exit $14.42 from `close_order_fill`,
pnl −$0.20, `pnl_status VERIFIED`, arithmetic matching. Run against a scratch
history file so production data stayed clean.

**Backfill.** Rebuilt the history from Alpaca's FILL activity feed — entries
corrected exactly by order id, exits matched lot-level FIFO. Reconciles to
broker cash flows within **$0.04** across the book. A first attempt using
order-level FIFO gave +$141 against a broker figure of −$8,228; the gap was
the uncorrected entry prices plus a missing options multiplier.

**Options are ×100.** Activity `price` is the per-share premium; cash flow is
`qty × price × 100`. Confirmed two ways: Alpaca's own `cost_basis` (5 × $0.72 ×
100 = $360), and implied starting equity, which resolves to $99,996 ≈ the
documented $100,000 start only with the multiplier applied.

**Result.** [[victoria]]'s validator grades Jerry for the first time:
**FAIL** — 116 trades, WR 35.3%, PF 0.972, Sharpe −0.005, net −$599.29,
ruin 0.0%. A real verdict on real data, replacing the NO_DATA it correctly
returned while the feed was empty. Jerry is now a sound foundation for the
queued crypto build.

# Part 4 — ICT/ICC strategy research (Lens)

Incomplete (still-forming) candles are dropped at download — an incomplete

**Instrument discrepancy flagged:** Jase's brief lists Blade as SPX500/DE30/XAU;

## 14. The lookahead test caught a real bug — mine

`ict_backtest.py` originally guaranteed close[1] structurally by handing each
detector a `bars[:i+1]` slice. That copy is quadratic and had to go for a 190k
-bar series. Removing it moved the guarantee from *structural* to *by
discipline*, so it needed proving.

`test_lookahead.py` destroys every bar after `i` (inverted highs/lows, absurd
prices, year-2099 timestamps) and asserts the detector's signal is
byte-identical.

**It failed on the first run.** `silver_bullet` produced a short signal with an
entry of −496,652 against poisoned data where the real series produced nothing.
Cause: my own earlier "fix" widened the FVG search to `disp_i .. disp_i+2`
unconditionally — and when `disp_i == i` that reads bars i+1 and i+2. The window
is now capped at `i`. An FVG completing after the decision bar is not knowable
yet; it gets picked up on a later bar instead.

Now: **2,107 detector calls compared, all identical.** Also verified zero
incomplete candles cached and that swing confirmation respects its k-bar lag.

That bug would have inflated every FVG-based model and been invisible in the
output.

## 15. Three engine defects found before that

- **FVG search too narrow** — originally checked only the exact displacement
  bar, missing most real imbalances (3 trades in 3 years).
- **Target selection cancelled the RR floor** — "target the next liquidity
  pool" took the *nearest* pool above entry, usually a few ticks away, so the
  1:2 floor then rejected 157 of 158 otherwise-valid setups. Now walks outward
  to the nearest pool that *also* clears 1:2, honouring both rules.
- **Degenerate stops** — a 0.73-point stop on SPX (ATR ~5) sized to a 1.5% loss
  on a 0.75% risk budget. Stops under 0.5 ATR are now rejected.

## 16. Cost model

Real per-candle spread (half each side), entry slippage 0.02 × ATR, **stop-exit
slippage 0.05 × ATR** (a stop is a market order fired into the move that
triggered it), commission 0.5 bps of notional per side. Position sized to 0.75%
risk off the real stop distance.

**Pessimistic fill rule:** if one bar's range contains both stop and target, the
STOP is assumed hit first. Without tick data the sequence is unknowable, and
assuming the good outcome is how backtests flatter themselves.

## 17. Verdict gate

Verdicts come from the SHARED `scripts/risk_validation.py` — the same gate Lens
and Victoria apply to live agents; a backtest does not get a friendlier
standard. Floor raised to **100 trades** per the brief (the validator's own
floor is 20). Below that: `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, no free-text grade.

Drawdown uses the FIXED math (anchored at the real starting balance), and a
model must be profitable in at least half its calendar years or it is
`MARGINAL` — regime-dependent, not validated.

## 19. Two methodology findings

**Compounding artifact.** `total_pnl` compounds position size off a growing
balance with fractional units and no liquidity cap. turtle_soup/DE30 reports
**$57.8M from $10k** — arithmetic, not a forecast. At FIXED $75 risk/trade the
same model returns **~$84k over 8 years** (~30% CAGR). `expectancy_R` and
`fixed_size_pnl` were added to every row; use those.

**The gate has a trade-frequency bias.** It FAILs anything with per-trade
Sharpe < 0.5 — mean P&L half a standard deviation *per trade*, a bar essentially
no real strategy meets, and one that structurally penalises high-frequency
models. Annualised (per-trade x sqrt(trades/yr)), turtle_soup scores **2.4-2.8**
while failing the per-trade test.

**The gate was NOT changed.** Moving a threshold after seeing results is how a
backtest talks itself into deployment. Reported as a finding for Jase to rule on
instead; `sharpe_annualised` is recorded alongside, clearly labelled advisory.

## 20. Recommendation

Nothing to deploy. If one model deserves further work it is **turtle_soup** —
but note what it is: the variant that *skips* MSS and displacement. Its edge may
be simple post-sweep mean reversion rather than ICT structure at all, which is
worth knowing before building more ICT machinery on top of it. The honest next
step is a walk-forward and parameter-sensitivity pass on turtle_soup alone, plus
a decision from Jase on the Sharpe basis.
